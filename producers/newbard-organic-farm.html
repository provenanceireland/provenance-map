Origin

Where exactly does this come from?

Newbard Farm sits at Cooladalane Lower, outside Lismore in Co. Waterford, less than 30km from Dungarvan. Siobhán Hubbard and Ross Newcombe have farmed this land since 2012, starting with a small group of pigs and expanding gradually into a certified organic operation raising heritage breed pigs, lamb, La Bresse chicken, and seasonal crops including pumpkins, kale, peas, oats, and an orchard.

What are the farming practices?

All animals are raised outdoors on pasture that is never sprayed with herbicides or insecticides. The pigs are Oxford Sandy & Black crossed with Tamworth — heritage breeds selected for outdoor hardiness, temperament, and meat quality — alongside Berkshire pigs. Gut health is managed through diverse pasture and selected anthelmintic plants rather than routine chemical worming. Crops grown on the farm — pumpkins, kale, peas, and oats — are fed directly to the animals. The farm also receives sourdough offcuts from Dún Artisan Bakery in Dungarvan, fed back to the pigs as part of a documented waste-reduction loop with a named local business.

What is in it?

Pork from heritage breed Oxford Sandy & Black x Tamworth and Berkshire pigs raised outdoors on certified organic pasture. Lamb and La Bresse chicken raised on the same farm to the same standard. No hormones. No routine antibiotics. Formally third-party certified organic — not a self-declaration. Nothing enters the supply chain from this farm that cannot be traced back to a named animal on a named field.

Why does this meet the Provenance standard?

Siobhán and Ross started farming in Lismore in 2012 with a few pigs and a determination to farm honestly. Over more than a decade they have built a certified organic operation with named breeds, named pasture, and documented practices. Siobhán is also leading a national campaign to reinstate farmer-led local abattoirs across Ireland — because a farm can produce as well as it chooses, but without local processing the chain between farm and plate cannot be closed. The farm is named. The farmers are named. The breeds are named. The practices are certified. That is the Provenance standard.

The Farmers

Siobhán Hubbard & Ross Newcombe

Siobhán and Ross began farming at Cooladalane Lower in 2012, starting with a small group of pigs and, in their own words, "a lot of questions, and a determination to farm in a way that felt right." Over the years that followed, those questions produced answers: certified organic status, heritage breeds chosen for outdoor hardiness and flavour, and a farm that grows its own feed crops and closes the loop with named local businesses.

Siobhán is a member of Talamh Beo, the Land Workers' Alliance of Ireland, and is leading a national effort to restore small-scale, farmer-led abattoirs across Ireland. The closure of the local abattoir that had served small producers in the south east left farms like Newbard with longer transport distances, higher costs, and greater animal stress at the point of slaughter. Rather than absorb the problem, she began organising a farmer-led alternative — securing Department of Agriculture funding, conducting HACCP research, and working with more than 160 farmers across the country who are ready to process locally as soon as conditions allow.

Newbard pork is stocked at Ardkeen Foodstore in Waterford, Ballymaloe Farm Shop and Cookery School, and Seagull Bakery in Tramore. The farm also supplies Dún Artisan Bakery in Dungarvan, who feed their sourdough offcuts back to the pigs in return.

"We need to bring back the local producers because so many people have lost the connection with food."
— Siobhán Hubbard, Irish Times, 2026
