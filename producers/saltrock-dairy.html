Saltrock Dairy Farm

Gorey, Co. Wexford

Non-homogenised Pasteurised Milk

Saltrock Farm is a 70-acre coastal farm in North Wexford naturally suited to grass growth. Catherine and Paul Kinsella produce non-homogenised, pasteurised milk and sell direct to the customer, bypassing every step of the industrial dairy chain that sits between a Wexford cow and the person drinking its milk.

The Visit

Origin

Where exactly does this come from?

Saltrock Farm sits on Tara Hill in North County Wexford. The herd of Holstein Cross cows graze outdoors for up to nine months of the year on the coastal pastures of North Wexford, being brought indoors when weather dictates, where they are housed on straw bedding and fed with silage harvested from the farm in the summer months. The farm address is Kildermot, Gorey, County Wexford.

How is it made?

Saltrock Dairy produces fresh, non-homogenised, pasteurised milk and handmade farmhouse butter, as well as yoghurt and kefir. The milk is freshly pasteurised but not homogenised, meaning it is not mechanically forced through tiny pores to break down the fat globules the way industrial dairy processors do. The cream sits at the top. The milk tastes the way milk from a real farm tastes.

What is in it?

Fresh whole milk from grass-fed Holstein Cross cows on named coastal Wexford pasture. Pasteurised. Non-homogenised. Nothing added. Nothing removed beyond the heat treatment required for safety. Sold direct to the customer, a fully sustainable system using reusable glass bottles and a self-service vending unit, completely waste-free.

Why does this meet the Provenance standard?

Paul Kinsella grew up on Tara Hill on his family farm and established his own farm in 1993. He converted to dairy in 2013, growing a strong healthy herd producing the quality product that inspired him and Catherine to sell direct to the consumer. After selling more than 35,000 litres of milk and claiming Green Business of the Year at the Gorey Business Awards, Catherine has already outperformed her first-year targets. The farm is named. The herd is named. The practices are documented. The founders are real people whose faces and farm appear in this profile. That is the Provenance standard.

The Farmers

Catherine & Paul Kinsella

Catherine spent thirty years as a nurse before launching Saltrock Dairy. During a week housebound due to COVID she made a decision, drew up a plan and paid a deposit on machinery. That was the beginning of Saltrock Dairy.

A growing dismay at the disparity between the quality of milk produced on almost every dairy farm in Ireland and what arrives in the supermarket bottle inspired Catherine to set up her own independent dairy food company. The disparity, she believes, is due to processes involved in industrial scale milk processing, a level of interference that is incompatible with the simplicity of the quality product being produced on almost every dairy farm in Ireland.

Catherine is passionate about the health benefits of returning to original milk products. Her commitment to local food production, sustainability and creating a circular economy continues to contribute to the growing food culture of Wexford and beyond.
